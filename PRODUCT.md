# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

No single primary audience — the site is genuinely mixed: recruiters and employers in data/tech, peers in data science and maths, and friends or personal contacts who want to follow along. Content should read honestly to all three rather than being tailored to one.

## Product Purpose

A personal site for Oli, a maths graduate moving into data science and analytics (currently at Lloyds Banking Group, previously DESNZ). It serves three overlapping purposes at once: a public accountability log that pushes him from passively consuming content to actively producing it (weekly writing, in first-draft rather than polished-thesis form), a professional showcase of technical and writing ability, and a personal archive of notes and thinking. Success isn't one metric — it's the habit of publishing sticking, and the site reading as credible when it needs to.

## Positioning

Someone genuinely curious and still figuring it out — writing honestly as a real intellectual journey unfolds (maths → data → industry), not performing finished expertise. The site's job is to make that process visible and legible, not to look like a polished thought-leader blog.

## Brand Personality

Earnest, warm, understated. Confidence comes from being honest about being early and unfinished, not from projecting authority. References: [y1d2.com](https://y1d2.com) and [neelnanda.io/about](https://www.neelnanda.io/about) — both personal, text-forward, quietly confident sites that let the writing carry the page rather than leaning on visual polish or credentialing.

## Anti-references

Generic "data scientist portfolio" templates: hero-metric stat blocks, card-grid project showcases, gradient accents, or anything that reads as a polished personal-brand funnel rather than an honest, in-progress body of writing.

## Design Principles

- Legibility of the writing comes first — the design should recede so the text and thinking are what's remembered, not the interface.
- Earnestness over polish — first-draft, unfinished, honest framing is a feature, not something to disguise with a slicker design.
- One voice across registers — technical notes, personal essays, and hobbies (music etc.) should feel like the same person, not siloed sections with different tones.
- No hard funnel — there's no primary conversion action to optimize for; the site rewards browsing, not a single CTA path.
- Leave room to grow — a CV/resume page is planned; navigation and information architecture shouldn't assume today's page set is final.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA baseline: solid color contrast, full keyboard navigation, and reduced-motion support. No additional or specialized requirements beyond good default practice.
