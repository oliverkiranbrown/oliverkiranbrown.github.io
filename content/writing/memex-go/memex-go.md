I love writing. And have been meaning to make it a daily habit for years now. The platonic ideal of me involved a beautiful, studied, gentle reflection on whatever happens to catch my attention, each morning, between a considered morning coffee and guided mediation. Like hell has that happened! 

Job is busy, friends to be seen, fun to be had, life to be lived. That is not stopping! But for me writing *is* thinking. And there's a hell of alot to think about at the moment. The world is in flux. 

And if I think back to some essays I wrote in lockdown (some of which can never see the light of day), it's pure considered thought. Taking on the January 6th Capitol riots, songs that moved me, films that lit me up. Working through the world. It's just the antidote to this brain-soupefying world of slop that we're swimming in. 

My late grandpa taught me this. He was an old soul, keeping a commonplace book—a treasure-trove of these (often hilariously eccentric) bites of brilliance. Through his writing process—which I still follow ever since he taught me—he published 184 articles. Starting with his career-switching PhD he completed in his 50s, this spawned a fruitful writing journey, with a regular column 'Bottled Brown' in *The Industrial Pharmacy*, and various books and speaking occasions. He loved the challenge of writing. Iterating till you find the right words to get at *it*. For me? An inspiration [who'll live on](../ode-to-gramps).

But my commonplace book will never be as meticulously organised as his. The modern equivalent is the blog. And it has benefits, forcing you to wrap your incoherent warblings into a form with enough clarity for you to slap your name on it and let others peruse. 

I've known these truths, but after been spellbound by some of Cory Doctorow's writings and public speaking, understanding that his secret sauce was just [The Memex Method](https://pluralistic.net/2021/05/09/the-memex-method/) whilst listening to [a conversation](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/fisher-price-management-with-cory-doctorow/id1737204926?i=1000784448965&uo=4) between him and another incredible articulate blogger Nikhil Suresh was enough. His incredible ability to string multiple intricate points together from various obscure recesses of the tech policy world, and then bring them to life into a single wonderfully funny, engaging, well-rehearsed story suddenly makes complete sense: he's blogged it! Iteratively, over years, slowing building up the ideas. Giving himself the time and space to crystallise his thinking. 

After wrestling with various ideas about AI in one mega-essay, it's certainly time to break-up this thinking process. Baby steps please! If I can approximate just a 50th of Doctorow's output, I'll be happy. Here's to that! Let the Memex begin...
