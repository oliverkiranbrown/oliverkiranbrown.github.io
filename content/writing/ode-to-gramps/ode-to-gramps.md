I loved my Grandpa. He wore his eccentricity on his sleeve. A multifaceted man: [pharmacist, sociologist](https://www.chemistanddruggist.co.uk/analysis/opinion/a-victorian-chemists-corking-christmas-mixture-UQUVVIZJKVGO3HGUSHWFHCORAU/), and wannabe philosopher right to the very end. 

I wrote this short piece when he passed, reading it at his funeral in Beccles, with some tears. He touched my life, and his impact will live on. 

Here's to a life well-lived ❤️

---

**To Grandpa**

People say, when you pass away, you're gone, forever. 

I don't think that's true. 

For me, Malcom was an inspiration. He wrote and wrote. Bottled Brown, Winning Words, even Gobble Gobble — a kids book about those pesky ducks in his pond! Hundreds of published works. And as I grew up, Malcom was always there to guide my writing. 

In his cosy study, surrounded by reams of paper, he taught me to love the messy process of creation. How to get your ideas out from your mind onto the page, into a story that moves people. And he taught me to believe in the power of my own unique voice. 

I see so much of my grandpa in myself. He encouraged me to read widely, he showed me how to argue philosophically and that it's OK to be deeply curious about the world. So for me, Grandpa isn't gone. He lives on in the flow of my hand over the paper as I write and the questions I ask when I don't understand something. 

Because his curiosity knew no bounds. When Grandpa was born in 1944, there was no personal computer, there was no internet, no artificial intelligence. And at each step, while some buried their head in the sand, Malcom leapt into the future, embracing each new technology as it came.

We'd have these starry-eyed conversations about AI discovering new proteins, doing the work of 3-year PhDs in mere hours. Visions of mapping out humanity's knowledge, and drawing out hidden connections, that spark new discoveries. So, as I begin my career as a Data Scientist, and I use the tools that filled Malcom with such wonder, I'll hold on to that sense of child-like excitement that he felt, every day. 

This year, I wrote a piece and I sent it to my Grandpa, as I always do, when I complete a piece of work I'm proud of. And other than encouraging me to write more (which I will!), he said these kind words:

>Life has its ups and downs. My take is that you have done all the right things and exceedingly well... You are free to do whatever you want to do and be whatever you want to be. You will thrive.
>
>With love and admiration,
>
>Grandpa

And, I do have a sense of "whatever I want to be". For me, Grandpa offers a north star of success. A vision of a life well lived, of someone who cared deeply about the world, and worked hard to make it a better place. Touching the lives of so many others as he did. 

So, finally, I invite you to reflect. In which ways has Malcom touched your life? In which ways has Malcom made you the person you are today? He's certainly inspired me. 

So you're never gone, really. 

My grandpa lives on. In the minds, body and practice of all of us.
